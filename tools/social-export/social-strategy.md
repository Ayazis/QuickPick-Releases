# QuickPick social creative strategy

## Revised direction after pilot review

Persistent branding: keep the same background and fixed logo/name/website/badges visible across both text and demo segments. Only the central message or demonstration changes. Use the real QuickPick logo rather than a substitute hexagon glyph. Requested badges: Free and Open source; the latter is draft launch copy until the source is publicly released under its planned license.

Latest refinement: separate reading from watching. Use a full-screen, static feature message, remove it completely, then play its demonstration. Repeat for the three features, followed by a dedicated CTA. Do not change headlines while asking the viewer to follow the cursor. The v3 cut uses 1.5–2 seconds per feature card, 3 seconds per demonstration, and a 2.5-second closing card (17 seconds total).

Lead with a short, multi-feature acquisition ad: three concrete benefits in roughly 12–15 seconds, with large animated headlines and cuts aligned to sound accents. Show the breadth and immediacy of QuickPick before asking for a download. Retain single-feature stories as follow-up explainers. The initial volume pilot felt too slow and instructional for the desired brand impression. `social-montage-post.md` describes the revised 12.5-second, three-feature draft.

Goal: a viewer who has never seen QuickPick should understand the problem it solves, what it is, and where to try it from a single post.

Product explanation: QuickPick puts your Windows apps and controls at your cursor.

## Story format

One familiar frustration → a visible shortcut/menu interaction → a visible result → one CTA. Use a recognizable desktop task before zooming into the action. Introduce the product within the first few seconds. Keep action fast and allow reading time for the explanation. Avoid logo-only intros and arbitrary speed-ups.

Use 15–22-second feature stories as the starting point. Build the combined introduction from selected moments once the individual stories work, rather than concatenating every full demo.

## Initial series

| Feature | Hook | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volume | Music too loud? | Reduce volume and resume the same task |
| App switching | Which window was it again? | Preview and choose the intended window |
| Per-app menus | Your browser and editor need different shortcuts | Relevant controls for each app, using the same hotkey |
| Customization | Put your most-used actions under one shortcut | Arrange recognizable actions, then use one |
| Workflow | Do this setup every morning? | Launch an app, run a command, insert routine text |

Start with volume, app switching, and per-app menus. Test two hooks per concept, holding the rest of the video constant. Compare early retention, completion, and visits/downloads within the same platform under similar posting conditions.

## Visual and production rules

- 1080×1920, 60 fps master; create alternate layouts when a placement needs them.
- Timed, short captions; sufficient context when muted.
- Show the cursor, shortcut, and outcome clearly. De-emphasize background content during close-ups.
- Keep critical messages away from likely UI overlays; verify the actual placement preview before publishing.
- Mark reconstructed interface demonstrations as illustrated.
- Optional sound reinforces the action; it must not be required to understand it.
- A single timestamp drives the scene, camera, cursor, and gauges. Output frame rate alone is not a synchronization check.
- Deliver MP4, silent MP4, poster, and matching post copy as a review package.
